Pad shift-register board

The smallest clean layout for the Quantum pad rebuild. Every pull-down, both caps and both chips fit on 21 × 10 holes, with a free hole between nets that must never touch.

Build order

  1. Cut and mark the boardCut 23 holes wide × 12 tall. The layout uses the inner 21 × 10, so the outer ring stays empty and no pad sits on a cut edge. Put a marker dot next to the top-left used hole (c1 r1) on both sides. When you flip the board it shows up top-right, as in the bottom view.
  2. Chip socketsU1 in c3–c10, U2 in c12–c19, legs in rows r4 and r7. Both notches point left, toward column 1. Column 11 stays empty between them. Solder two opposite corner legs, press the socket flat, then do the rest. Sockets mean you never heat the chips, and you can test before they go in.
  3. 17 resistors and 2 caps, standing upBend one leg back down beside the body like a hairpin so each part takes two holes, one above the other. Resistors go in r1+r2 above the chips and r9+r10 below them, plus one in c21 r9+r10 for Mode. Caps go in c3 and c12, rows r1+r2. Ceramic caps can go either way round. Clip the legs after soldering and keep the offcuts for step 5.
  4. Solder bridges, undersideSwitch to the bottom view. Each input column gets one blob across three pads: chip leg, wire hole, resistor leg (r4→r3→r2 up top, r7→r8→r9 below). The same goes for the cap columns, LOAD and CLK (c3, c4, c12, c13), DATA c10 r4→r3, the chain ends c9 and c19 r4→r3, Mode c21 r7→r9, and the power link c1 r3→c3 r3. Bridge only along a column. Never let solder join two columns side by side.
  5. Bare wires, undersideLay a straight offcut along r1 from c2 to c18 and solder it at every pad with a leg: that's the top ground bus. Do the same along r10 from c5 to c21. The 3.3 V rail runs down c1 from r2 to r9. Then add five short ground runs: c4 r4→r1 and c13 r4→r1 (pin 15 of each chip), c18 r4→r1 (chip 2 pin 10), and c10 r7→r10 and c19 r7→r10 (pin 8 of each chip).
  6. Check with no power Must passSet the meter to beep mode and touch the 3.3 V rail (c1) and the ground bus (r1). It must stay silent. If it beeps, look between c3/c4 and c12/c13, where 3.3 V and ground sit next to each other. Next, in resistance mode, each button hole (r3 and r8) should read about 10 kΩ to ground. Finally, beep each button hole against its neighbours: no beeps.
  7. Five insulated jumpers, top sidec3 r8 → c12 r8 carries LOAD to chip 2. c4 r8 → c13 r8 carries CLK to chip 2. c19 r3 → c9 r3 is the chain from chip 2's output into chip 1. c1 r4 → c12 r3 carries 3.3 V to chip 2. c11 r1 → c11 r10 joins the two ground buses. They can pass over the chips.
  8. Wires off the boardPush each wire in from the top and solder it underneath. That's 6 to the SuperMini, 16 buttons, the Mode button, and the button commons, which all go to the 3.3 V pads c1 r5–r9. The tables below list every one.
  9. Power check, chips still out Must passWith the battery switch OFF, plug in USB. Black probe on c2 r1, red probe on c3 r4 and then c12 r4 (pin 16 of each socket): both should read about 3.3 V. Each button hole should read 0 V released and 3.3 V pressed.
  10. Chips in, then testUnplug the USB. Press both 74HC165s into their sockets with the notch toward column 1, plug back in, and open the tester at localhost:8765. Press every button and move both sticks, and I'll check the readings.

Parts

  • Perfboard offcut, 23 × 12 holes
  • 2 × 74HC165. They must be HC: LS and HCT parts don't work at 3.3 V
  • 2 × 16-pin DIP sockets (recommended)
  • 17 × 10 kΩ resistors (bands: brown, black, orange)
  • 2 × 10 µF ceramic capacitors
  • Thin insulated wire for 5 jumpers and about 25 off-board wires

Firmware

This layout uses the same chip pins as the current firmware, so nothing needs re-flashing if you wire it as shown. If a button ends up on the wrong hole, leave it: I'll swap it in software after the press test.
