Pad shift-register board · 10 × 9

Everything on a 10 × 9 offcut. The chips sit on top. All 16 pull-downs and both caps stand on the underside. Their top legs are joined by one bare wire in the air, and that wire is ground. Button wires don't get holes: they're soldered flat onto the chip-leg pads.

How this fits

Each pull-down needs a spot right next to its chip leg. On top, the only free holes are the outer columns and the gap row. Underneath, though, the holes under each chip body are free. So every resistor stands on its pad under the chip, with its short leg bridged to the chip leg beside it. That's 16 resistors in four rows of four, one hole apart. Their tall legs all bend into one bare wire, and that wire is ground. The price is thickness and fiddly soldering. Test before the chips go in.

Build order

  1. Mark the boardHold it 10 holes wide and 9 tall. Put a marker dot by the top-left hole (c1 r1) on both sides. When flipped, it's top-right.
  2. Sockets first, on topU1 in c2–c9 with legs in r1 and r4. U2 in c2–c9 with legs in r6 and r9. Both notches face column 1. Row 5 stays empty as the gap. Solder all legs before anything goes underneath.
  3. 3.3 V padsUnderneath, bridge c1 r1–r3 together and onto c2 r1 (U1 pin 16). Then bridge c1 r6–r8 together and onto c2 r6 (U2 pin 16). The Mode button skips the board: it wires straight to SuperMini GPIO20 and GND, using the chip's built-in pull-up.
  4. Underside parts, one row at a timeFor each resistor, cap or ground post, cut the short leg to about 3 mm and bend it into an L. Stand the part on its pad and solder the foot flat so it runs onto the chip-leg pad next to it. That one joint holds the part and makes the bridge. Keep the bodies upright, tops level, and leave about 1 mm between bodies. The ground posts are resistor-leg offcuts standing in c3 r2, c9 r3, c3 r7, c8 r7 and c9 r8. Leave each part's long top leg sticking up for now.
  5. Air busBend every top leg under U1 toward one straight bare offcut lying across them, and solder each one. Do the same under U2. Join the two buses with a short insulated wire. This whole cage is ground. Watch columns 2 and 3: the cap's foot (3.3 V) and the ground-post foot sit side by side there.
  6. Check with no power Must passSet the meter to beep mode. The 3.3 V pads (c1 r1) against the air bus must stay silent. Each button pad should read about 10 kΩ to the bus. Neighbouring button pads must not beep against each other.
  7. JumpersOn top, run 3.3 V from c1 r3 to c1 r6. Underneath, using insulated wire soldered flat: LOAD from c2 r4 to c2 r9, CLK from c3 r4 to c3 r9, and the chain from c9 r6 to c8 r1.
  8. Off-board wiresThe 3.3 V input and three button commons go through holes in column 1. Everything else is soldered flat onto its pad: buttons onto their chip-leg pads, DATA, LOAD and CLK, and GND onto the air bus. Pre-tin the wire tip, hold it on the pad and touch it with the iron for a second. Any extra commons go flat onto the 3.3 V pads too.
  9. Power check, chips still out Must passBattery switch OFF, then plug in USB. Put the black probe on the air bus and the red probe on c2 r1, then on c2 r6: both should read about 3.3 V. Each button pad should read 0 V released and 3.3 V pressed.
  10. Chips in, then testUnplug the USB. Push the chips in with the notch toward column 1, plug back in, and open localhost:8765.
